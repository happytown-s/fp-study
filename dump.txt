  1: The set of optimal portfolios offering the highest expected return for each level of risk
  2: The boundary between risky and risk-free assets
  3: The minimum investment needed for diversification
E: The efficient frontier, developed by Markowitz, shows optimal portfolios that maximize expected return for a given risk level. Portfolios below the frontier are suboptimal; those on the frontier offer the best risk-return tradeoff.

--- Q178 [Risk Management] ---
Q: 'Value at Risk' (VaR)は何を意味するか。
  0: The total value of an investment portfolio
  1: The maximum expected loss over a given time period at a given confidence level
  2: The average return of a portfolio
  3: The probability of a market crash
E: VaR estimates the maximum loss with a given probability over a specific time. Example: 1-day 95% VaR of 1 million yen means there is a 95% chance of losing no more than 1 million yen in a single day.

--- Q179 [Risk Management] ---
Q: ' dollar-cost averaging's key advantage in risk managementは何か。
  0: It guarantees profits in any market
  1: It reduces the impact of timing risk by spreading purchases over time
  2: It eliminates all investment risk
  3: It maximizes returns during bull markets
E: Dollar-cost averaging reduces timing risk by purchasing at regular intervals regardless of price. It naturally buys more units when prices are low and fewer when high, smoothing the average purchase price over time.

--- Q184 [External Economics] ---

  2: An increase in the value of foreign currency
  3: A decrease in government spending
